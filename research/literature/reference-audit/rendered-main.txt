   ABSTRACT Stateful stream-processing systems must adapt resource allocations while preserving result
   correctness, service latency, and recovery requirements. Additional diagnostic evidence can help a controller
   distinguish resource pressure from other bottlenecks, but gathering evidence and waiting for analysis also in-
   cur operational costs. Reconfiguration can introduce further disruption while state is transferred or restored
   and accumulated events are processed. This study proposes EcoStream-Agent, a supervisory controller that
   acquires operational evidence according to its expected effect on the intervention decision. The planned
   design separates language-model interpretation of diagnostic evidence from numerical estimation of action
   effects and transition costs, with execution restricted to supported interventions. The research question is
   whether decision-focused investigation improves resource efficiency sufficiently to outweigh collection
   and delay overhead. The intended evaluation uses controlled stateful streaming workloads, common
   action spaces, numerical and diagnostic-agent baselines, deterministic evidence-processing alternatives,
   and measurements of allocated resources, service quality, backlog clearance, and controller cost. This pre-
   experiment draft develops the comparative literature review; implementation, experimental results, and
   empirical conclusions remain pending. Neither publication novelty nor physical energy savings have been
   established.

   INDEX TERMS Agentic AI, diagnostic evidence acquisition, resource efficiency, stateful stream process-
   ing, systems optimization.
