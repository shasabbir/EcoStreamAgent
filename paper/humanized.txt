abstract:
Stateful stream-processing systems need to cope with changing resource allocations with the same result correctness, service latency and recovery requirements as they did in their previous deployments. Even with that information, other diagnostic information can be used to determine if a resource is the problem or if another bottle neck exists, but that information must be collected and analyzed, and there is an operational cost to do so. Reconfiguration can cause additional disruptions during the transfer and/or restoration of state, and processing of accumulated events. In this work, a supervisory controller called EcoStream-Agent is proposed which acquires the operational evidence based on its effect on the intervention decision. The design is planned to split up the interpretation of diagnostic evidence with the numerical estimation of the effect of actions and the transition costs, and to only execute supported interventions. The research question is posed to determine if action sensitive investigation is able to provide an improvement in the efficiency of resources which is worth the cost of collection and delay overheads. The planned evaluation is done with controlled stateful streaming workloads, common action spaces, numerical and diagnostic-agent baselines, deterministic action processing alternatives, and measurements of allocated resources, service quality, backlog clearance, and controller cost. This is a draft copy of the pre-experiments literature review; results of implementation, experimental results and empirical conclusions are yet to be seen. No publication novelty nor any physical energy savings has been proven.

introduction:
Distributed stream-processing systems always have high-rate event sequences flowing into them, and they always have low-latency requirements for the transformation of these event sequences. Many streaming operators provide stateful processing for windows, joins, aggregations, and event-time coordination that is not provided by stateless request processing. This stateful state has consequences in this state: changing parallelism or placement may necessitate a state move or checkpoint restore, record reordering, or replay. Elasticity, scheduling, state consistency and fault tolerance are not independent concerns, but are interrelated when it comes to stream-processing architecture and resource management. Studies of transactional and fault-tolerance also reveal that correctness is dependent on the interplay between processing semantics, state management, checkpointing and recovery protocol.

The operator is required to provide sufficient capacity to support the offered load, meet latency goals and bounce back from disturbances without infinite accumulation of input. Backlog is received input that has not been processed and a service level objective (SLO) is an operational requirement, like a latency, availability, or recovery limit. Therefore, restoring a job is not the same as restoring service: if the rate of recovering capacity is the same as the rate of the arriving jobs then the recovered job will still be behind the arriving job. Recovery-aware controllers, as well as controllers and optimizers for multiple configurations, already take care of significant aspects of this resource-service-quality tradeoff. State-preserving reconfiguration, partitioning, multi-objective tuning, heterogeneous-resource selection and predictive scaling are all features of more recent controllers.

Operating signal that leads up to an SLO violation is usually not clear. Insufficient capacity, load skew, communication overhead, slow worker, checkpoint interference, state transfer and recovering backlog can all cause the latency to increase. These causes may need different intervention, some may not be remediable with merely additional resources. Communication and skew are already handled by stream schedulers and partitioners, and research on recovery and checkpointing shows that the extent of disruption of an intervention is based on state size, protocol and recovery scope. A controller that doesn't recognize these differences can overprovision, perform an inappropriate reconfiguration or waste time investigating an incident where there is no supported action.

Modern observability systems provide metrics, logs, traces, topology, configuration history and job level diagnostics tools. Agentic root-cause-analysis systems can determine which tools to invoke, as well as integrate multiple modalities and make diagnostic conclusions. But the collection and analysis of additional evidence comes with a cost. Queries can use up API or compute capacity, a longer diagnostic sequence can slow down intervention and adding more context can result in higher costs for the language-model without altering the action taken. On the other hand, premature termination may lead to the choice of inappropriate repair. Diagnostic confidence in itself is therefore an incomplete control objective as the value of evidence is related to the ability to change a feasible intervention and the operational outcome.

EcoStream-Agent is proposed for the study of this decision boundary. A diagnostic observation gathers information about the job's current operating state and an intervention alters the job or resources assigned to it. The study also distinguishes between the choice of the intervention and the implementation of the intervention. The proposed controller would employ language-model reasoning only to interpret the bounded diagnostic evidence and to formulate the supported hypotheses, estimates of action effects, SLO risk, resource use and transition cost would be done by a numerical decision-layer and a guard would restrict the execution to a predefined action catalogue. An unsupported incident would result in no change or escalation not a made up repair.

The crux of the issue is whether the potential benefit of intervening based on another observation is more than an observation that will cost and the cost of delay. The planned decision rule is going to use correctness and declared SLOs as constraints, and compare feasible actions using time-integrated resource use and measured transition/recovery overhead. The evidence-collection latency, the use of tools and the agent/API cost will be charged to the same operational episode and will be reported separately; if a scalar trade-off is used by the controller, it will be declared, and sensitivity-tested. This framing is not the same as maximizing diagnostic accuracy alone: if two hypotheses predict the same safe action, there might be no need to distinguish between them; if a hypothesis is of low probability, there might be a great need to distinguish between it and other hypotheses, even if the other ones are correct. This distinction will be tested under matched evidence tools and action spaces, instead of assuming an agentic architecture to be beneficial.

A. Research questions
The study has four research questions.

RQ1 (decision value): When the same actions can be performed by the action-sensitive evidence acquisition, a fixed-budget diagnostic agent, deterministic evidence processing, and numerical configuration controllers, and their evidence is identical at the outset, would the action-sensitive evidence acquisition choose the best interventions?

RQ2 (operational efficiency): Is EcoStream-Agent able to reduce the resources allocated and consumed, while maintaining the correctness of the results, the latency SLOs and backlog-clearance criteria, after accounting for evidence-collection, decision-delay and stateful transition cost?

RQ3(mechanism attribution):What is the proportion of the effect of the observed effect due to the evidence-acquisition and stopping policy, language-model interpretation, and transition-cost model? This is not a question which can be answered by comparing just the system, but needs component ablations.

RQ4 (robustness and limits): When does the proposed policy help, when does it become redundant, and when does it break? Unsafe proposals, unnecessary actions, escalation behaviour, investigation overhead will be recorded in the evaluation along with average performance.

B. Planned contributions
The study aims to make the following contributions: a stateful-stream-control formulation for cost-aware acquisition and stopping, where evidence is valued by its expected effect on a feasible intervention, rather than solely by confidence (both subject to implementation and empirical validation); a guarded EcoStream-Agent architecture which produces a separation of bounded language interpretation, numerical action scoring, transition-cost estimation, and supported execution; a reproducible evaluation protocol with controlled stateful workloads, correctness and backlog oracles, matched action spaces, strong numerical and diagnostic-agent baselines, and explicit accounting for controller, evidence, and reconfiguration overhead; and an empirical characterization of conditions under which agentic investigation improves operational decisions and conditions under which cheap telemetry, deterministic logic, or direct numerical control is sufficient.

These are research products that are anticipated to occur, but not necessarily completed research products. The problem formulation and comparative literature review are present in the present manuscript, the controller, frozen protocol, measurements and conclusions supported by evidence are yet to be done. The working title reflects the intended research direction and does not imply that any potential savings in resources and/or novelty of publication were achieved.

Literature review:
The relevant literature relates to stream-processing foundations, numerical resource optimization, stateful reconfiguration, fault recovery, performance evaluation, cost-aware information acquisition and agentic operational diagnosis. These strands already offer plenty of mechanisms needed by an adaptive streaming controller. This review is not meant to view the two capabilities in combination as automatically novel, but to differentiate between the capabilities that are inherited and the proposed evidence-acquisition question. There are forty-one papers either published or in press in the bibliography. The 10 most similar technical precedents are listed in the table of comparison in the manuscript, while the rest of the basic and cross-cutting studies are included thematically below. The comparisons are not controlled reruns of published systems, but are comparisons of mechanisms that are inspected and the evaluation scope.

There are a number of surveys that give supporting bases. Liu and Buyya structure resource management around the concepts of provisioning, operator parallelization and scheduling with service and cost constraints. Fragkoulis et al. relate stream-processing evolution to state, ordering, fault tolerance, elasticity, and reconfiguration and Cardellini et al. categorize the adaptation strategies and open issues at runtime. Fault-tolerance mechanisms in stream-processing systems are surveyed by Wang et al., while Zhang et al. survey transactional stream processing, and the consistency implications of the combination of transactions and continuous execution. These sources all drive the need to think of adaptation as a standard systems problem with boundaries of semantics, state, recovery, scheduling and service quality. They are surveys, which are used to define the terminology and coverage, but do not represent a controller baseline that is ready to be executed.

Autoscaling and configuration optimization using A.Using A. for autoscaling and configuration optimization.
Phoebe is a workload forecasting, parallel profiling and quality of service (QoS) tool to tune streaming workloads for latency and recovery. While the recovery discussion sets a useful upper bound on capacity (the capacity that matches normal input), this may still be inadequate to clear the backlog following a failure. Instead Daedalus constructs processing-capacity models based on observations at runtime and proactively horizontally scales based on the forecasted capacity. It shows via comparisons that reduction of resources can be a latency tradeoff, not all service metrics can be enhanced at the same time.

Demeter takes into account multiple objectives: forecasting and multi-objective Bayesian optimization; it extends the set of options to worker count, CPU, memory, processing slots, and checkpoint interval. Already retrieves extra profiling runs, based on information gain, and skips profiling that is not necessary. Thus, the field of selective acquisition of information in general is not an untouched research field. The candidate distinction for EcoStream is related with operational diagnostic observations to differentiate among the conflicting bottleneck explanations, not extra executions to learn the performance of a configuration.

More recent work expands upon the idea of adaptation and takes it away from single job parameters. Workload prediction, evolutionary configuration tuning and operator scheduling are coordinated across pipeline ensembles by STREAMLINE, which provides a state-preserving update mechanism, as detailed in its publisher abstract. MUDAP makes elasticity parameters of services and resources available and the RASK agent coordinates competing services by using structural knowledge, regression and a constrained solver. Several MUDAP actions affect the quality of the model or service. This flexibility is helpful in its context, but cannot be equated to changing resources while maintaining an exact streaming query. EcoStream is then required to compare controllers with the same actions and semantics - with a strong low overhead numerical alternative.

Scarf offers a very timely Flink comparator. It is a combination of workload-sensitivity based knob selection, multi-objective reinforcement learning, and transferable graph-based models to tune throughput and resource usage. According to its publisher deposited abstract, it is an offline learning of configuration trade-offs and online selection. So, learning resource performance trade-offs or transferring configuration knowledge is nothing new. Adapted Scarf baseline shall state the differences in knobs, training effort and workloads available.

There are also strong precedents for prediction and heterogeneous resource choice numerically. MMP-SPS is a method that performs monitoring, predictor selection and adaptation in separate control windows in Apache Storm and compares prediction-error and bandit-based selection. It also evaluates resource saving without any service degradation. A controller that coordinates application and operator level controllers for heterogeneous nodes is proposed by Russo Russo et al. using the techniques of Bayesian optimization and reinforcement learning. In Li et al., they offer a CEBFD approach to pair resource-demand prediction and cost-aware executor placement for Spark Streaming. The latter's "micro-batch" execution cannot be replaced by the proposed Flink setting, but the separation of demand prediction from placement cost is relevant.

ProOSStream integrates offline scheduling optimization, federated reinforcement learning for online adaptation and workload forecasting in the edge-cloud setting. It expands upon the comparison to a centralized scaling approach, and importing its distributed approach to a single-host study would not provide an equivalent deployment evidence. The works are useful for testing to find out if diagnostic acquisition adds value on top of accurate prediction and competent numerical scheduling and includes learning and controller overhead.

Vogel et al. show that there is a well known alternative to agent based runtime adjustment: online and transparent self-adaptation of parallel stream patterns. Li et al. both take scheduling cost and topology of networks into account in distributed stream computing. These studies limit the potential claim of novelty: EcoStream is not the first to think of adapting parallelism, or to take topology into account. Instead, the comparison should determine if diagnostic evidence alters action selection beyond the ability of a capable numerical controller to make inferences based on the controller's monitored state.

Evaluation methodology is also included in the comparison. ShuffleBench separates the activity of shuffling large amounts of data, and compares distributed stream-processing frameworks in controlled workloads. It's not an autoscaler, but it does show why throughput / latency of one engine and topology shouldn't be considered as a universal feature. For experiments run on EcoStream, the following parameters should be reported: framework, serialization, partitioning, state, offered load and network conditions, and engine-specific effects should not be blamed on the controller.

A. Partitioning, Scheduling and Stateful reconfiguration
Performance degradation may not be due to inadequate CPU allocation alone but also may be caused by problems in data distribution or communication or by problems in the mechanics of adaptation. Ra-Stream is a combination of resource scaling, communication-aware subgraph partitioning and fine-grained task scheduling. When the input changes, SAP-DPO dynamically partitions, adapts its update cycles and adjusts the sliding window. These methods encourage separating the capacity pressure from distribution related issues for interpretation of streaming telemetry. They also limit a wide novelty claim to that of adaptive monitoring or update frequency.

CAOM identifies the bottleneck operators from runtime data and determines the time of migration, taking into account the accumulation of data in case of interruption. Therefore there are already direct precedents of the streaming of bottleneck-sensitive interventions and the migration costs depending on time. DRRS deals with the problem of stateful scaling, which involves re-routing, record re-ordering and state subdivision of migrating state. Unlike picking a configuration, this is something to lessen the disruption when a configuration has already been picked (scaling operation).

It is important for the transition model of EcoStream. All stateful adjustments do not need to be costed as full restarts; model should show the true path of execution of the chosen engine and configuration. Similarly, the identification of the skew or impaired worker is not effective remediation unless the common action catalogue has an intervention that can help address the skew. If they are not in that case catalogue, then a no change or escalation outcome must be stated, not that the person had their cases repaired.

Further scheduling and recovery efforts further solidify this boundary. To reduce task- and node-level stragglers, Ms-Stream coordinates grouping, partitioning and placement. Whereas Ns-Stream is focused on near source processing in the form of dynamic grouping, having published its implementation in Storm. Both give information about capacity and distribution bottlenecks, but are not directly available in an untouched Flink executor. Any incident that cannot be distributed (without assistance) is out of scope for a remediation claim or should be explicitly escalated.

The scope of recovery is one of the design variables. Takdir et al. introduce partial snapshots and local recovery in Flink, which recover a subset of operators instead of the whole Flink job. A-FP4S parallelly distributes state fragments across a peer-to-peer overlay network and parallel recovery adapts to hardware heterogeneity. These are mechanisms that set up alternatives to global restoration. Nothing would be invented by EcoStream, these are just ways of recovering that are supported by the system, and the measurements can only be based on real restart, replay and backlog costs of the deployed configuration.

Those costs are further impacted by checkpoint protocol and execution architecture. CheckMate provides a comparison of coordinated, uncoordinated and communication-induced checkpointing for streaming dataflows under various workload conditions. Scabbard is an example of single-node fault-tolerant stream processing, and shows an example of a recovery design whose assumptions are different than those of distributed Flink recovery. These works warn against the simple imposition of a 'generic' transition penalty on all interventions. The cost model of EcoStream should be tailored to the protocol and deployment of each experiment and the results cannot be extrapolated to other recovery architectures that were not evaluated.

A diagnosis and thinking in terms of tools (C. Agentic diagnosis)
Interestingly, RCAgent is a pretty close precedent since autonomous operational diagnosis has been used with Alibaba Cloud Flink jobs. It provides its controller with tools for collecting information and takes control of long observations via snapshot keys, corrects malformed output and can conclude a diagnosis. So the presence of an autonomous tool selection, efficient context management or a stopping action is not enough to characterize EcoStream. The proposed difference relates to the criteria for acquiring evidence and stopping, and how that will impact executed interventions.

The ORACL is a combination of language-model root-cause reasoning and resource-allocation recommendations. It has verified manuscript delegates who perform numerical forecasting in another component and an interface for verification. Diagnosis before allocation, as well as separation of numerical prediction from language interpretation are therefore design features that were inherited. The planned comparison by EcoStream should not be about an agent being able to come up with an allocation recommendation, but about whether another observation changes the preference for feasible action enough to make it worth the effort.

Evidence of streaming-control performance isn't provided by scientific tool agents. SciToolAgent is a knowledge-graph assisted tool for retrieving and selecting scientific tools and executing scientific tasks, along with safety checks. BioMedAgent breaks up the planning, coding and execution, allowing interactive exploration and retrieval of successful experiences to build biomedical workflows. They are relevant only in context of the tool contracts, feedback and experience organization. If a large tool graph or other agent roles are added to a small operational catalogue, they should not be added unless there is evidence that the added complexity of the tools helps to make decisions. It is a hypothesis that success in biomedical or scientific workflows can be transferred to latency sensitive streaming control.

The need for a precise comparison is enhanced by more recent diagnosis work. The scope published by TAMO explicitly merges LLM diagnosis and multimodal observations in cloud-native systems by using tools. LocaleXpert translates metrics, logs and traces into language-based descriptions and leverages the local presence and expertise of specialist agents to localize and interpret failures. MA-RCA distinguishes diagnostic tasks and adds retrieval grounding and runtime validation of hypotheses. This means that the use of tools, multimodal evidence, specialist agents and hypothesis checking are not “sufficient contribution statements” in themselves, but existing capabilities. Reconcile in detail with TAMO's inaccessible final method is still required.

CausalRCA offers a solution that is not based on LLMs, teaching a weighted causal graph from metrics and ranking fine-grained causal candidates. This kind of diagnostic ranking alone does not assess the benefit of a repair performed, and the assumptions of an observational causal model need to be verified. The diagnosis quality, the selection of the action, the overhead of the investigation and the service recovery after the intervention should be measured separately for the proposed study. It should not take a more convincing explanation as an indication of a better operational result.

Multimodal diagnosis is earlier than the latest LLM agents. Nezha conducts fine-grained root cause analysis on multimodal observability data that is interpretable. DiagFusion is the fusion of metrics, logs, traces, deployment information, event representations, and graph learning, to help localize faulty instances, and classification of failure type. These systems are based on the idea that different modalities might offer complementary evidence, but also offer strong non-agentic comparators. Thus, for EcoStream, it is important to evaluate the utility of sequential, action-sensitive acquisition, not just a single-metric threshold, versus fixed multimodal fusion.

Cost-aware sequential observation is also a well-known problem outside the field of operational diagnosis. The active-feature-acquisition literature formalizes the selection of the next observation to be acquired and the decision on when to terminate it, using model-based, model-free, and model-based and model-free policies to balance the predictive loss with the acquisition cost. So, EcoStream can't boast of the novelty of cost aware acquisition or stopping in general. Its smaller question is whether an observation is worth making if it is to be done while the robot is functioning; if it has a chance of changing the preferred feasible intervention following state-transition and recovery costs.

D. Sustainability and the measure of boundaries
Flink also has implemented energy-aware scheduling. Li et al. suggest an SLA-based scheduling strategy along with an inter-node and intra-node load balancing approach. The motivation for the scheduling of the abstract has been reviewed and found to be suitable but the instrumentation details are not sufficient to allow for an independent physical-energy assessment. So resource allocation and energy consumption should therefore always be differentiated quantities in comparisons.

Colangelo et al. show how to orchestrate AI workloads with a grid-oriented approach with power control, pausing and resource reallocation, while adhering to performance-flexibility tiers. This is a precedent for physical-measurement, not a direct streaming-controller baseline – some AI-job service promises allow for some level of throughput reduction, while some exactly query evaluation proposals, such as EcoStream's, maintain semantics along with offered load. Each of the metrics (allocated core-seconds, consumed CPU, instantaneous power, total energy and carbon emissions) responds to various questions. The present study will aim at Resource efficiency and Service quality as these are the elements that can be measured without physical instrumentation and sustainability is a driving force only.

A gap and proposed study question for E. is shown below:
Changes are required to the reviewed systems for the development of proactive adaptation, constrained optimization, selective profiling, autonomous diagnosis, and recovery- or migration-aware execution. Their strengths inspire a particular unanswered question for this study: When is it worthwhile to pay for an extra observation of a stateful intervention in terms of the costs of collection and waiting? It is not a statement that all previous controllers don't have decision-sensitive acquisition, but rather a research question.

EcoStream would explore an acquisition and stopping policy based on expected 'intervention loss'. The proposals for its evaluation should distinguish between this policy and the contribution of language interpretation and the transition-cost modelling with matched diagnostic tools and actions. The use of a conventional diagnostic agent and deterministic processing of the same evidence is necessary controls. If the benefit it is proposing is a small one, if it can be found through a cheap telemetry, if it is a rule that is deterministic and corresponds to the agent, or if the benefit is lost due to lapse of time, it may not be a good one. These are potential outcomes for the study and not exceptions that are cut from the study post testing. The specific mechanism is given priority pending final versions and implementations, which is the closest.

The forty one paper bibliography focuses on recent papers dealing with methods and includes older published work when it provides a foundation in the areas of resource-management, fault-tolerance, self-adaptation, benchmarking, multimodal-diagnosis, or evidence-acquisition. Several records were examined using abstracts from the publishers, and/or manuscripts of the authors; no attempt was made to resolve method passages that were not available, or differences from finished published records. Published versions (not ordinary preprints) are mentioned in the bibliography. Only literature that has been inspected is described, and results reported in the literature are not included in this draft nor presented as EcoStream results.
