\title{EcoStream-Agent: Action-Sensitive Evidence Acquisition for Stateful Stream Control}

\author{Author details pending}

\markboth{EcoStream-Agent}{EcoStream-Agent}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Stateful stream-processing systems need to adapt resource allocations while preserving result correctness, service latency and recovery requirements. Even with that information, other diagnostic information can be used to determine if a resource is the problem or if another bottle neck exists, but that information must be collected and analyzed, and there is an operational cost to do so. Reconfiguration can cause additional disruptions during the transfer and/or restoration of state, and processing of accumulated events. In this work, a supervisory controller called EcoStream-Agent is proposed which acquires the operational evidence based on its effect on the intervention decision. The design is planned to split up the interpretation of diagnostic evidence with the numerical estimation of the effect of actions and the transition costs, and to only execute supported interventions. The research question is posed to determine if action sensitive investigation is able to provide an improvement in the efficiency of resources which is worth the cost of collection and delay overheads. The planned evaluation is done with controlled stateful streaming workloads, common action spaces, numerical and diagnostic-agent baselines, deterministic evidence-processing alternatives, and measurements of allocated resources, service quality, backlog clearance, and controller cost.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Agentic AI, diagnostic evidence acquisition, resource efficiency, stateful stream processing, systems optimization.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{D}{istributed} stream-processing systems continuously ingest high-rate event sequences and transform them under low-latency requirements. Many streaming operators provide stateful processing for windows, joins, aggregations, and event-time coordination that is not provided by stateless request processing. This state has consequences for adaptation: changing parallelism or placement may necessitate a state move or checkpoint restore, record reordering, or replay. Elasticity, scheduling, state consistency and fault tolerance are not independent concerns, but are interrelated when it comes to stream-processing architecture and resource management~\cite{liu2020resource,cardellini2022adaptation,fragkoulis2024evolution}. Studies of transactional and fault-tolerance also reveal that correctness is dependent on the interplay between processing semantics, state management, checkpointing and recovery protocol~\cite{wang2021faulttolerance,zhang2023transactional}.

The operator is required to provide sufficient capacity to support the offered load, meet latency goals and bounce back from disturbances without infinite accumulation of input. Backlog is received input that has not been processed and a service level objective (SLO) is an operational requirement, like a latency, availability, or recovery limit. Therefore, restoring a job is not the same as restoring service: if post-recovery processing capacity only matches the input arrival rate, the recovered job may remain permanently behind. Recovery-aware controllers, as well as controllers and optimizers for multiple configurations, already take care of significant aspects of this resource-service-quality tradeoff~\cite{geldenhuys2022phoebe,geldenhuys2024demeter}. State-preserving reconfiguration, partitioning, multi-objective tuning, heterogeneous-resource selection and predictive scaling are all features of more recent controllers~\cite{pfister2024daedalus,russorusso2023hierarchical,pedratscher2025streamline,liu2026scarf}.

Operating signal that leads up to an SLO violation is usually not clear. Insufficient capacity, load skew, communication overhead, slow worker, checkpoint interference, state transfer and recovering backlog can all cause the latency to increase. These causes may need different intervention, some may not be remediable with merely additional resources. Communication and skew are already handled by stream schedulers and partitioners~\cite{wu2025msstream,fan2026rastream,ren2026sapdpo}, and research on recovery and checkpointing shows that the extent of disruption of an intervention is based on state size, protocol and recovery scope~\cite{theodorakis2021scabbard,siachamis2024checkmate,takdir2025localrecovery,qing2025drrs}. A controller that doesn't recognize these differences can overprovision, perform an inappropriate reconfiguration or waste time investigating an incident where there is no supported action.

Modern observability systems provide metrics, logs, traces, topology, configuration history and job level diagnostics tools. Agentic root-cause-analysis systems can determine which tools to invoke, as well as integrate multiple modalities and make diagnostic conclusions~\cite{wang2024rcagent,zhang2023diagfusion,yu2023nezha,zhang2025tamo}. But the collection and analysis of additional evidence comes with a cost. Queries can use up API or compute capacity, a longer diagnostic sequence can slow down intervention and adding more context can result in higher costs for the language-model without altering the action taken. On the other hand, premature termination may lead to the choice of inappropriate repair. Diagnostic confidence in itself is therefore an incomplete control objective as the value of evidence is related to the ability to change a feasible intervention and the operational outcome.

EcoStream-Agent is proposed for the study of this decision boundary. A diagnostic observation gathers information about the job's current operating state and an intervention alters the job or resources assigned to it. The study also distinguishes between the choice of the intervention and the implementation of the intervention. The proposed controller would employ language-model reasoning only to interpret the bounded diagnostic evidence and to formulate the supported hypotheses, estimates of action effects, SLO risk, resource use and transition cost would be done by a numerical decision-layer and a guard would restrict the execution to a predefined action catalogue. An unsupported incident would result in no change or escalation not a made up repair.

The crux of the issue is whether the potential benefit of intervening based on another observation is more than an observation that will cost and the cost of delay. The planned decision rule is going to use correctness and declared SLOs as constraints, and compare feasible actions using time-integrated resource use and measured transition/recovery overhead. The evidence-collection latency, the use of tools and the agent/API cost will be charged to the same operational episode and will be reported separately; if a scalar trade-off is used by the controller, it will be declared, and sensitivity-tested. This framing is not the same as maximizing diagnostic accuracy alone: if two hypotheses predict the same safe action, there might be no need to distinguish between them; a low-probability hypothesis can matter when taking the wrong action under that hypothesis would be expensive. This distinction will be tested under matched evidence tools and action spaces, instead of assuming an agentic architecture to be beneficial.

\subsection{Research Questions}
The study has four research questions.

\textbf{RQ1 (decision value):} When all methods receive the same initial evidence and can execute the same actions, does action-sensitive evidence acquisition select better interventions than a fixed-budget diagnostic agent, deterministic evidence processing, and numerical configuration controllers?

\textbf{RQ2 (operational efficiency):} Is EcoStream-Agent able to reduce the resources allocated and consumed, while maintaining the correctness of the results, the latency SLOs and backlog-clearance criteria, after accounting for evidence-collection, decision-delay and stateful transition cost?

\textbf{RQ3 (mechanism attribution):} How much of any observed effect is due to the evidence-acquisition and stopping policy, language-model interpretation, and transition-cost model? This is not a question which can be answered by comparing just the system, but needs component ablations.

\textbf{RQ4 (robustness and limits):} Under which workload shifts, state sizes, skew patterns, recovery conditions, and unsupported incidents does the proposed policy help, become unnecessary, or fail? Unsafe proposals, unnecessary actions, escalation behaviour, investigation overhead will be recorded in the evaluation along with average performance.

\subsection{Planned Contributions}
Subject to implementation and empirical validation, the study aims to make the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a stateful-stream-control formulation for cost-aware acquisition and stopping, where evidence is valued by its expected effect on a feasible intervention, rather than solely by confidence;
    \item a guarded EcoStream-Agent architecture which produces a separation of bounded language interpretation, numerical action scoring, transition-cost estimation, and supported execution;
    \item a reproducible evaluation protocol with controlled stateful workloads, correctness and backlog oracles, matched action spaces, strong numerical and diagnostic-agent baselines, and explicit accounting for controller, evidence, and reconfiguration overhead;
    \item an empirical characterization of conditions under which agentic investigation improves operational decisions and conditions under which cheap telemetry, deterministic logic, or direct numerical control is sufficient.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper presents a problem formulation and comparative literature review positioning the proposed approach against recent technical precedents.
